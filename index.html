    Job Market Paper

    Asymmetric Information and Capital Regulation in SME Lending: A Structural Model of Bank and Non-Bank Competition

    Joint with Felipe Netto (Bank of England).
    Presentations: ECB Conference on Financial Stability and Macroprudential Policy 2026 (scheduled), 2026 EBA Policy Research Workshop (scheduled), 16th Workshop of the Empirical Financial Intermediation Network (scheduled), Rising Scholars Conference in Finance 2026 (University of Zurich).
    [ | Draft]

    We study how competition between banks and non-banks in UK unsecured SME lending is shaped by bank-only risk-based capital requirements, differences in screening abilities, and their interaction. Combining confidential loan-level data with hand-collected data on lenders' screening channels, we document that banks screen loans either automatically or through relationship managers, while non-banks screen only automatically. Motivated by this pattern, we estimate a structural model of lending and find that non-banks assess risk more precisely when both lenders screen automatically, but banks become more precise once they switch to human screening. In counterfactuals, tighter capital requirements raise bank rates less under human screening, where more precise screening lets banks retain safer, less capital-intensive borrowers. Moreover, fully automating banks' screening expands access to credit, but the loss of precision raises banks' default rates.
