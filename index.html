Research

Job Market Paper

"Information Asymmetry, Intermediary Capital, and the Aggregate Cost of Credit" JMP

Sole-authored

Submitted

► Abstract

This paper examines how information asymmetries between commercial banks and capital markets shape the equilibrium cost of external finance and aggregate investment dynamics. Using a novel matched dataset of syndicated loan originations and borrower balance sheets, I document that loan spreads are significantly higher for informationally opaque firms, even after exhaustive controls for observable risk. I develop a general equilibrium model in which banks acquire costly private information, and show that the resulting wedge between intermediated and market-based finance generates amplification of monetary policy shocks and endogenous credit cycles consistent with U.S. data. A calibrated version of the model accounts for approximately 40 percent of the observed volatility in the external finance premium over the business cycle.

"Rollover Risk and the Maturity Structure of Corporate Debt"

with Co-Author Name (Co-Author Institution)

Revise & Resubmit, Review of Financial Studies

► Abstract

We study how rollover risk shapes firms' optimal debt maturity choices and the macroeconomic implications of these choices. In a dynamic capital structure model with stochastic refinancing costs, firms exposed to high rollover risk optimally shorten their debt maturity during credit contractions, amplifying the initial shock. Using a large panel of U.S. public firms matched to TRACE bond-transaction data, we document a strong negative relationship between rollover-risk exposure and average debt maturity, consistent with the model. Counterfactual analysis suggests that endogenous maturity shortening amplifies output contractions by approximately 15 percent in a typical recession.

"Dealer Balance Sheet Constraints and the Pricing of Corporate Bonds"

with Co-Author A (Institution A) and Co-Author B (Institution B)

Under review

► Abstract

We provide evidence that fluctuations in primary dealer balance-sheet capacity have economically significant effects on corporate bond prices and secondary-market liquidity. Using confidential supervisory data on dealer inventories matched to FINRA TRACE transaction records, we identify exogenous variation in intermediary capacity via regulatory constraints and trace its effects on bid-ask spreads, price impact, and expected excess returns. A one-standard-deviation tightening of dealer constraints raises effective spreads by 42 basis points and lifts expected monthly excess returns by 14 basis points. Effects are concentrated in high-yield and illiquid bonds and are amplified during episodes of market stress.
